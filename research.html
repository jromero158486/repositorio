Advisor: Dr. Anastasia Yendiki Deep learning for fiber-bundle segmentation in high-resolution connectomics.

  • Built multimodal representation-learning modules combining microscopy and tracer-derived signals to enhance robustness and cross-brain generalization in fiber-bundle segmentation.
  • Developed preprocessing and cross-brain registration pipelines enabling inter-subject anatomical alignment; introduced spatial continuity and topology-aware metrics to evaluate segmentation fidelity.
  • Explored structure–function relationships by linking high-resolution anatomical features with large-scale network representations, improving interpretability in neuroimaging-based connectomics.
  • Led the end-to-end thesis project: dataset curation, modeling, evaluation, and iterative refinement based on weekly LINC Lab presentations and expert feedback.

Advisor: Dr. Cindy Hsin-Liu Kao Multi-agent LLMs for physiological signal interpretation.

  • Engineered a multi-agent LLM framework to interpret multimodal physiological signals (PPG, IMU, Audio), integrating few-shot prompt engineering, environment-aware signal processing, and in-context reasoning. Validated through synthetic stress scenarios and comparative baselines.
  • Designed and implemented sensor data pipelines for biosignal acquisition and real-time feedback, combining microcontroller-based sensing, preprocessing modules, and cloud-integrated inference. Optimized latency and signal fidelity for closed-loop adaptive responses in wearable prototypes.
  • Conducted literature review and usability prototyping in affective computing, multimodal signal processing, and HCI. Prototyped emotion-aware wearable interfaces and evaluated usability through heuristic testing, informing co-creation workshops and participatory user studies.

Advisor: Dr. Jing Han Deep learning for fetal phonocardiography.

  • Designed and implemented a hybrid pipeline for respiratory sound analysis, combining signal processing (cycle segmentation, spectral feature extraction, ensemble-based detection) with LLMs for natural language querying and verification.
  • Applied audio processing and ML frameworks (Librosa, SciPy, scikit-learn, PyTorch, TensorFlow) to detect multi-event patterns in the ICBHI dataset for respiratory event classification.
  • Developed verification mechanisms integrating structured textualization with consistency checks to mitigate LLM hallucinations in clinical queries.
  • Conducted literature review and manuscript drafting, formulating research questions, designing the methodological framework, and authoring the first draft of the paper.

Advisor: Dr. Katia Vega Biosensing wearables for cortisol monitoring.

  • Designed and prototyped biosensing systems for real-time cortisol monitoring, integrating microfluidic sensors and embedded electronics into functional on-body wearable form factors.
  • Applied probabilistic machine learning methods to classify stress levels from multimodal physiological data, improving predictive accuracy and enabling uncertainty quantification for robust stress detection.
  • Evaluated usability and feasibility of wearable prototypes through iterative testing, providing design insights for human–computer interaction and informing pathways for real-world deployment.

Advisor: Dr. Gerald Adam Zwettler Retrieval-augmented generation for clinical document understanding.

  • Developed a RAG pipeline for multimodal clinical documents (structured text, imaging reports, unstructured PDFs), leveraging advanced chunking strategies and metadata-aware indexing to improve context retrieval and reduce information loss.
  • Integrated multilingual LLMs (English and German) for clinical entity extraction and summarization, using spaCy for preprocessing, HuggingFace Transformers for fine-tuning, and custom Chain-of-Thought prompting for reasoning transparency.
  • Conducted iterative evaluations with clinicians to refine interpretability and usability, incorporating domain feedback into pipeline redesign and ensuring reproducibility with Git and optimized PyCharm workflows.

Advisor: Dr. David Cory Resonator design and Bayesian modeling for quantum biosensors.

  • Investigated magnetic resonance principles for next-generation quantum biosensors, designing novel resonator architectures and applying Bayesian modeling to quantify and mitigate uncertainties in magnetic field interactions.
  • Built and validated mathematical models and simulations using Python, MATLAB, Mathematica, and COMSOL, optimizing calibration protocols and reducing computation time for iterative experimental design.
  • Contributed to hardware development by assisting in the design of circuits and prototypes for experimental integration, bridging theoretical models with practical implementation in NV-diamond quantum systems.
  • Validated resonator prototypes with preliminary experimental measurements, comparing results against simulation predictions to ensure alignment between theoretical and practical performance.

Advisor: Dr. Gilberto Ochoa-Ruiz Multi-instance learning for kidney stone classification.

  • Designed and implemented a multi-instance learning (MIL) framework for automatic classification of kidney stones from medical imaging, leveraging Keras and TensorFlow to handle weakly labeled datasets.
  • Integrated attention-based pooling mechanisms into the MIL architecture, improving both predictive accuracy and diagnostic interpretability by highlighting discriminative image regions.
  • Explored robustness and generalization under class imbalance through data augmentation strategies and cross-validation, ensuring reliability across heterogeneous imaging datasets.
  • Conducted ablation studies and comparative evaluations with baseline CNN models, demonstrating the advantages of MIL for clinical decision support in medical imaging workflows.

Advisor: Dr. Michelle Ploughman Kinematic biomarkers of multiple sclerosis using robotic assessment.

  • Conducted standardized clinical assessments with patients diagnosed with Multiple Sclerosis, using the Kinarm robotic platform to collect high-resolution kinematic and sensorimotor data.
  • Applied and compared machine learning algorithms (Random Forest, SVM, Neural Networks) with scikit-learn and TensorFlow to identify movement patterns and biomechanical correlates of upper-extremity dysfunction.
  • Performed feature engineering and validation analyses to link robotic metrics with traditional clinical tests, supporting the development of objective biomarkers for MS progression.
  • Contributed to a peer-reviewed publication validating robotic assessment as a quantitative biomarker of upper-extremity dysfunction in Multiple Sclerosis.
